# Course: Stop Your Divorce

**Status:** Spec in progress
**Domain:** TBD (stopdivorce.com?)
**Last Updated:** 2026-02-22

---

## ICP

Person who is 2–90 days from either spouse filing for divorce, OR 3–24 months post-separation
trying to reconcile. The primary target is the one who does NOT want the divorce.

Psychographics:
- Panicked, reactive
- May be begging, pleading, threatening, or alternating between all three
- No framework for what to do — acting on instinct
- Likely making the situation worse without knowing it
- Doesn't understand the relationship dynamics at play
- Desperate for a plan

---

## Start State

Panicked and reactive. No framework. Possibly begging or threatening. Doesn't understand
relationship dynamics. Likely making the situation worse. Feels powerless.

## End State

Grounded. Clear plan. Skills to execute it. Understands exactly what dynamic they and their
partner are in. Has taken full accountability. Has made a clear, dignified request to engage
in a repair process. Actively executing the repair.

---

## Limiting Beliefs (draft — to be refined)

1. "If I just explain myself well enough, they'll understand and come back"
2. "I need to act fast or I'll lose them forever"
3. "This is mostly their fault"
4. "Begging / pursuing shows I care and will win them over"
5. "I can't change — this is just who I am"
6. "If I give them space, it means I don't care"
7. "The relationship is broken beyond repair"

## Required Skills (draft — to be refined)

1. Recognize and exit pursuit-withdrawal dynamic
2. Take full accountability (without self-flagellation)
3. Regulate emotional state in high-stakes moments
4. Make a dignified bid for repair without pressure
5. Execute a structured repair process
6. Maintain boundaries and self-respect throughout

## Materials Produced

| Material | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `relationship_dynamics_profile` | context + output | RD assessment output — their dynamic, roles, toxic cycles |
| `pattern_analysis` | context | AI analysis of which toxic cycles are active and what role each person plays |

## Module Outline (draft — to be refined after Clear Story Audit)

### Module 1: Understanding What's Actually Happening
- Learning objectives: student understands the pursuit-withdrawal dynamic, identifies their
  own role, stops blaming and starts observing
- Belief addressed: "This is mostly their fault"
- Skill: Recognize and exit pursuit-withdrawal dynamic
- Key material produced: `pattern_analysis` v1, `relationship_dynamics_profile`

### Module 2: Full Accountability
- Learning objectives: student can distinguish accountability from blame or self-punishment,
  can write and speak their accountability without defense
- Belief addressed: "I need to explain myself" / "It's not all my fault"
- Skill: Take full accountability
- Key material produced: `statement_of_full_accountability`

### Module 3: Emotional Regulation in Crisis
- Learning objectives: student has at least one reliable self-regulation practice,
  can pause before reacting
- Belief addressed: "I need to act fast"
- Skill: Regulate emotional state in high-stakes moments

### Module 4: The Dignified Bid
- Learning objectives: student understands what a bid for connection is vs. a pursuit move,
  can make one clearly and without pressure
- Belief addressed: "Begging/pursuing shows I care"
- Skill: Make a dignified bid for repair without pressure
- Key material produced: `repair_request_letter`

### Module 5: The Repair Process
- Learning objectives: student knows exactly what steps to take, in what order, and why
- Belief addressed: "The relationship is broken beyond repair"
- Skill: Execute a structured repair process
- Key material produced: `repair_process_plan`

### Module 6: Maintaining Self Through the Process
- Learning objectives: student can hold their own identity and worth regardless of outcome
- Belief addressed: "I can't change" / "I need their response to be okay"
- Skill: Maintain boundaries and self-respect
